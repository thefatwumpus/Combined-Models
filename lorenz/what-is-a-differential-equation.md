# What Is a Differential Equation?

A differential equation is an equation that describes how something changes in terms of itself.

In math, "differentiation" is just a fancy word for measuring a **rate of change** (like speed or growth) through derivatives.

A differential equation connects a thing (like the amount of water in a tub) to its rate of change (how fast it's draining).

Imagine you have a savings account. Instead of an equation telling you exactly how much money is in the bank today, a differential equation tells you the interest rate rule:

- **Standard equation:** "You have $1,000." (fixed amount)
- **Differential equation:** "Your balance grows by 5% of whatever is currently in there every year." (rule for change)

In symbols, if $B$ is the balance, the rule is

$$\frac{dB}{dt} = 0.05\,B$$

In algebra you have numbers → numbers (ex. solving for $x$).

In calculus you have functions → functions (the derivative or integral of a function).

In differential equations you have relationships → functions.

A differential equation tells you a rule about how the function changes (its derivatives). The goal is to find the actual function that follows that rule.
